MEASURE BEFORE YOU CLAIM

How good are the recommendations?

A held-out test set and two kinds of evaluation keep us honest.

Rating errors use the 0.5–5 star scale. Lower RMSE and MAE are better.

Better ranking, with a popularity trade-off.

The original factor model slightly improves star-rating error but gives weak top-five results. The updated ranker combines its personal estimate with training rating counts. Validation selected a support weight of 1.6. It improves all three ranking metrics over the original model and movie-average baseline. Compared with the most-rated baseline, precision and NDCG improve modestly, while recall is slightly lower. These are descriptive results, not a statistical significance claim.

01 / Prepare

Sample 5,000 users with seed 42. Split each user's history chronologically into approximately 80% training, 10% validation and 10% test ratings.

02 / Select

Train four factor/regularization configurations. Select factor settings and early stopping using validation RMSE. Then select the ranking weight using NDCG@5 on 500 validation users.

03 / Evaluate

Freeze the ranking weight before evaluating the same historical test cohort of 200 users. This is a comparison on an already-used test set, not a fresh untouched benchmark.

The rating baseline uses user/movie biases. Ranking comparisons include both a Bayesian movie average and a most-rated baseline. The support bonus favors popular titles and may reduce novelty. Displayed star estimates are unchanged; they are distinct from the score used to order recommendations. Ranking relevance is a held-out rating of at least four stars; unobserved relevance is unknown. Per-user chronological splits are not a globally prospective backtest. These results evaluate existing users, not the new visitor hybrid. Visitor recommendations combine training-only item similarities and a smaller matrix-factorization preference signal; their quality has not yet received a held-out evaluation.
